# Case study: Incident Briefing Room

The Incident Briefing Room is the clearest Reactor example because its world
changes over time. The maintained state is not "write a report." It is "the
incident channel has an accurate current briefing."

Its continuity rule says to recheck on incident messages, on status-page changes,
and while the incident is active. Its maintained truth covers impact, timeline,
owner, next action, and customer-facing status. Its fulfillment boundary is to
summarize new facts, ask for missing owner input, and update the briefing.

This case study mirrors the v0.1 technical report. The numbers below are from
deterministic measurement worlds, not a hosted production system, and they are
not dollarized.

## A changing world

When the world changes, the Reactor spends fresh model work on the change and
reuses prior verdict work on the rechecks in between. One deterministic receipt
trail shows the shape:

| Beat | Cause | Evidence | Fresh / reused | Receipt | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| `12:00` | real input | initial incident feed | `37/0` | fresh | baseline briefing recorded |
| `12:15` | forecast recheck / evidence age | unchanged | `0/37` | memo hit | no material change |
| `12:30` | real input | `incident-opened` event | `37/0` | fresh | briefing changes |
| `12:45` | forecast recheck / evidence age | changed evidence, unchanged since | `0/37` | memo hit | updated verdict reused |

The two fresh beats are the two moments something actually moved: the bootstrap
and the `incident-opened` event. The rechecks in between hit the memoized
verdict because the input fingerprint did not move. A naive loop re-spends on
every scheduled turn whether or not anything changed.

## A quiet world

When nothing changes, the cost is not zero, and the honest claim is precise about
why. A quiet run has the same proof shape plus one extra plan-age audit floor:

```text
bootstrap          fresh        (real first render at boot)
evidence-age tick  memo hit     (nothing moved)
plan-age audit     fresh floor  (the forecast-amortized recheck)
evidence-age tick  memo hit     (nothing moved)
```

That floor is the deliberately-declared forecast cadence: the cost of not letting
a stale plan become quietly wrong. The cost thesis is **surprise plus a
forecast-amortized audit floor**, not surprise alone. It is still less fresh
model work than re-asking on every scheduled turn.

## What the measured rows show

The technical report measures two controlled scenarios against two controls: a
no-memo Reactor (same schedule, no memoization) and a non-Reactor naive loop
(rechecks on the same schedule with no receipts or memo keys).

| Scenario | Row | Model invocations | Fresh tokens | Reused tokens |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Static | Reactor | 2 | 46 | 46 |
| Static | Reactor, no memo | 4 | 92 | 0 |
| Static | Naive loop | 4 | 256 | 0 |
| Changing | Reactor | 2 | 74 | 74 |
| Changing | Reactor, no memo | 4 | 148 | 0 |
| Changing | Naive loop | 4 | 148 | 0 |

In the static scenario the Reactor does fresh work at bootstrap and at the
plan-age audit, while evidence-age rechecks hit memoized verdicts: 2 invocations
and 46 fresh tokens against 4 and 256 for the naive loop. In the changing
scenario the Reactor spends fresh work on the bootstrap and the material change,
then reuses verdict work on the rechecks: 2 invocations and 74 fresh tokens
against 4 and 148 for both controls.

The changing-world win is fresh model work plus reusable-verdict proof, not a
total accounted-token claim over the no-memo control.

## What this does and does not show

This is a deterministic, local demonstration. The Reactor maintains a long-lived
responsibility with runtime-produced receipts, reusable verdict proof,
forecast-paced rechecks, and explicit surprise attribution. It is not a hosted
production incident system updating real customer communications, and the figures
are measured token accounting, not dollar costs.
